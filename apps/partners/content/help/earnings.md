# Earnings: what circls will pay you

The **Earnings** page answers one question: **for the period I choose, how much money is actually coming to me?**

Every figure on the page is your **net payout** — what reaches you after circls' commission and the payment gateway's charges have come out, and after any refunds. It is deliberately not the total your customers were charged.

This matters more than it sounds. If you add up the amounts in an event's registrations table, or in a venue's bookings list, you are adding up **what each customer paid at checkout** — and a customer's bill includes charges that were never yours: the booking fee circls charges the customer, and the customer's share of the payment-gateway fee. Totalling that column will always give you a number higher than your payout. Earnings does the arithmetic properly, in one place.

Earnings covers all three ways you sell — **events, memberships and venues** — together and separately.

## Who can see it

Earnings is visible to **Owners**, **Managers** and **Read-only** members. **Staff** do not see it: they run the desk, and financial reports are not part of that. If your organisation is suspended you can still see what you are owed — suspension stops changes, not visibility.

## Choosing a period

Pick **Today**, **Last 7 days**, **Last 30 days**, **This month**, **Last month**, or **Custom** for any two dates.

**A sale counts on the day the customer paid.** So today's sales show up today, rather than waiting for a payout to be worked out. "Last 7 days" means seven whole calendar days including today.

Days are cut at midnight in the timezone shown under the buttons — the one picked in the timezone selector at the top of the portal, or your own if you have not set one. This only matters for a sale made late in the evening, which can fall either side of midnight depending on the zone you are viewing in.

## What the numbers are

**Net payout** — the headline figure. Everything circls owes you for sales in the period, across events, memberships and venues.

Underneath it, the same figure split three ways:

- **Events** — ticket and registration sales.
- **Memberships** — plan sign-ups and renewals.
- **Venues** — court and arena bookings.

Then **every event, plan and venue** listed individually with its own net figure, so you can see where the money came from. Use the **All / Events / Memberships / Venues** buttons to narrow the list, and **Export CSV** to take it into a spreadsheet.

**Paid out** — next to each net figure, how much of it has already been transferred to you. A dash means nothing has been sent for that row yet.

This will usually be lower than the net payout beside it, and for a recent period it will often be a dash. That is expected, not a shortfall: a sale counts on this page the day your customer paid, but the money itself moves on the weekly payout cycle. The gap between the two columns is simply what is still on its way to you.

**Rows fill in one at a time, not all together.** A payout is sent as separate amounts for each event, plan and venue it covers, and they are not always sent at once. So you may see one event's row fill in while another from the same week is still showing a dash — that does not mean anything is wrong with the second one, only that it has not been sent yet. A row stops short of its net payout until every part of it has gone out.

**Paid out can be higher than net payout, and that is not a mistake.** If you refund a customer after the payout covering their booking has already gone out, the refund lowers what the period is worth without taking back money that has already reached you. So Paid out stays where it is while Net payout drops. The difference is settled against a later payout, and the page says by how much.

If you sell in more than one currency, each one gets its own set of figures. They are never added together.

## Collected at your desk

Bookings you took yourself and marked as paid directly to you appear in their own section, **not** in the net payout figure.

That money never passed through circls, so circls has nothing to pay you for it — it is already yours. It is shown here so that this page and the **Dashboard** visibly account for each other rather than appearing to disagree, since the Dashboard's Revenue tile does include desk takings.

## When the money actually arrives

The **Paid out** column tells you how much has already reached you; the rest is still to come.

Payouts run on the weekly cycle set out in your [Partner Terms](/terms): circls transfers your collections for the week, net of refunds and commission. Timing can shift for holidays, bank delays or a risk review, and the parts of one week's payout can go out separately — which is why the rows on this page fill in individually rather than all at once.

So a sale you see on this page today is money owed to you now, and paid on the next cycle. If a row has been sitting on a dash far longer than that, or the two columns disagree with what reached your account, [report it from the Help Centre](/help) — the figures here are built from the same records your payouts are.

## Why a figure can be negative

A period can show a negative figure, and that is the truth about that period rather than an error.

It happens when refunds in the period outweigh sales in it — most obviously if you refund a booking that was sold last month. The refund is dated when you made it, so this month carries it. The difference is settled against a later payout.

One thing to know about refunds: **circls' commission on a sale is not returned when you refund the customer.** The customer gets their money back in full, and the commission on that original sale stays charged. So a sale that is later refunded in full leaves a small negative amount behind rather than netting to exactly zero.

## How this compares with the Dashboard

The two pages are answering different questions, and both answers are correct:

- The **Dashboard's Revenue** tiles show **money that moved** — the full amount customers paid, plus your desk takings. It is a measure of trade.
- **Earnings** shows **money coming to you** — net of everything, and excluding desk takings, which are already in your hands.

Earnings will therefore always read lower than the Dashboard's Revenue for the same period. The gap is circls' commission, the gateway's charges, and any desk cash — which is exactly what the separate desk section is there to make visible.

If a figure still looks wrong after allowing for all of that, tell us through **Questions** and include the period you are looking at.
